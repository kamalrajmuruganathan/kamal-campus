# Bac — Compréhension et expression écrites (anglais)

**Durée conseillée : 1 h 30 — /20.**

## Document

> In the city of Belfast, a long grey wall once separated two communities that
> did not trust each other. For years, it was simply a symbol of fear. Then,
> slowly, the wall began to change. Artists arrived with paint, ladders and
> ideas, and turned the cold concrete into an open-air gallery of murals.
>
> Some paintings told stories of the past — of conflict, of families divided, of
> people who had lost everything. Others looked towards the future, showing
> children playing together under bright, imaginary skies. The wall did not
> disappear, but it started to speak a different language.
>
> Grace, a local guide, walks tourists along the murals every week. "People think
> art is just decoration," she says, "but here, art is memory. It forces you to
> stop and look at your own history."
>
> Not everyone agrees on what the murals should show. A painting that celebrates
> one hero can feel like an insult to the neighbours on the other side. Art, in
> a divided city, is never neutral. It can heal wounds, but it can also reopen
> them.
>
> Still, Grace believes the murals do more good than harm. "A wall you can talk
> about," she says, "is better than a wall you are afraid of."

## Comprehension (10 points)

1. What did the wall in Belfast originally represent? (2 pts)
2. What are the two main types of stories shown by the murals? (2 pts)
3. Explain Grace's sentence: "art is memory". (2 pts)
4. Why does the text say that "Art, in a divided city, is never neutral"? (2 pts)
5. What is Grace's final opinion about the murals? Do you find her argument
   convincing? (2 pts)

## Written expression (10 points)

Choisis UN sujet et écris 130–150 mots :

- A. A tourist writes a blog post after visiting the Belfast murals. Write the post.
- B. "Public art can change the way a society sees its own history." Discuss this
  idea with examples.

## Corrigé indicatif

- **Compréhension** :
  1. Un symbole de peur, séparant deux communautés qui ne se faisaient pas confiance.
  2. Des œuvres tournées vers le passé (conflit, familles divisées) et d'autres vers
     l'avenir (enfants jouant ensemble).
  3. L'art conserve et rend visible l'histoire collective ; il oblige à regarder son
     propre passé.
  4. Parce que célébrer un héros d'un côté peut blesser l'autre communauté : l'art prend
     position.
  5. Elle estime que les fresques font plus de bien que de mal ; on peut discuter la
     nuance (mémoire vs division).
- **Expression** :
  - Attendus : lexique de l'art et de la mémoire (*mural, heritage, to commemorate,
    to divide, to reconcile*).
  - Sujet B : thèse nuancée, au moins deux exemples (street art, statues, monuments).
  - Connecteurs : *on the one hand / on the other hand, however, as a result, to sum up*.
