# Bac — Compréhension et expression écrites (anglais)

**Durée conseillée : 1 h 30 — /20.**

## Document

> Clara could have flown from Edinburgh to the south of France in less than three
> hours. Instead, she chose a train that took almost a full day. Her friends
> thought she was mad. "Why waste all that time?" they asked.
>
> But for Clara, the journey was not wasted time — it was the best part. From her
> window, the landscape changed slowly, like a film played in slow motion: grey
> Scottish hills, green English fields, the flat plains of France, and finally
> the first olive trees near the Mediterranean. You cannot see any of that from
> above the clouds.
>
> On the night train, she shared a compartment with a retired teacher from Wales
> and a young musician from Spain. They talked for hours, in a mixture of
> languages and laughter, about music, politics and the strange comfort of moving
> through the dark while the world sleeps.
>
> Of course, the train was slower, and sometimes late. Clara arrived tired, with
> a stiff neck and an empty coffee cup. But she also arrived with three new
> friends and a head full of images.
>
> "Flying takes you to a place," she likes to say. "A train takes you *through*
> the world. That is not the same thing at all."

## Comprehension (10 points)

1. What choice does Clara make, and how do her friends react? (2 pts)
2. According to the text, what can you see from a train that you cannot see from
   a plane? (2 pts)
3. Who does Clara meet on the night train, and what do they do? (2 pts)
4. What are the disadvantages of the train mentioned in the text? (2 pts)
5. Explain Clara's final sentence. What difference does she make between flying
   and taking the train? (2 pts)

## Written expression (10 points)

Choisis UN sujet et écris 130–150 mots :

- A. Write a page from Clara's travel journal describing one moment of the journey.
- B. "Slow travel is better than fast travel." Do you agree? Give arguments and
  examples.

## Corrigé indicatif

- **Compréhension** :
  1. Elle choisit le train (près d'une journée) plutôt que l'avion ; ses amis la trouvent
     folle et parlent de temps perdu.
  2. Le changement progressif des paysages : collines écossaises, champs anglais, plaines
     françaises, oliviers méditerranéens.
  3. Un enseignant gallois retraité et un jeune musicien espagnol ; ils discutent des
     heures de musique, politique, etc.
  4. Plus lent, parfois en retard ; elle arrive fatiguée, la nuque raide.
  5. L'avion mène *à* un lieu ; le train fait traverser *le monde* — l'expérience du
     trajet compte autant que la destination.
- **Expression** :
  - Attendus : lexique du voyage (*journey, landscape, compartment, to wander*),
    comparatifs et superlatifs.
  - Sujet B : au moins deux arguments (écologie, rencontres vs temps, coût).
  - Connecteurs : *whereas, although, in addition, all in all*.
