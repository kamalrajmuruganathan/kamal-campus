# Bac — Compréhension et expression écrites (anglais)

**Durée conseillée : 1 h 30 — /20.**

## Document

> The novelist had spent thirty years writing about a town that did not exist. He
> called it Greywater — a small port with a lighthouse, three churches and a
> harbour that smelled of salt and diesel. Readers loved it. They sent letters
> asking for directions, and some even planned holidays there.
>
> One rainy afternoon, a young woman knocked at his door. She had travelled a
> long way, she said, to see Greywater with her own eyes. The writer, embarrassed,
> explained gently that the town was pure invention. There was no lighthouse, no
> harbour — only sentences on paper.
>
> The woman was quiet for a moment. Then she smiled. "But I grew up there," she
> said. "When my father was ill, I read your books to him every night. That town
> is where we were happy. You may have invented the streets, but you did not
> invent what happened to me inside them."
>
> The writer never forgot her words. For years he had believed that fiction was
> the opposite of reality — a pleasant lie. Now he understood something stranger
> and truer: an imaginary place can hold very real memories.
>
> "A story," he later said, "is not a lie about the world. It is a home the mind
> can walk into whenever the real world locks its doors."

## Comprehension (10 points)

1. What is Greywater, and how do readers react to it? (2 pts)
2. Why does the young woman travel to visit the writer? (2 pts)
3. Explain the woman's argument: "You may have invented the streets, but you did
   not invent what happened to me inside them." (3 pts)
4. How does the writer's idea of fiction change during the story? (2 pts)
5. Comment on the final metaphor about the "home the mind can walk into". (1 pt)

## Written expression (10 points)

Choisis UN sujet et écris 150–180 mots :

- A. Write the letter the young woman sends to the writer after her visit.
- B. "Fiction is more than entertainment: it can be as meaningful as real life."
  Do you agree? Discuss with examples from books, films or series.

## Corrigé indicatif

- **Compréhension** :
  1. Une ville portuaire imaginaire (phare, trois églises, port) inventée par le
     romancier ; les lecteurs l'adorent au point de vouloir la visiter.
  2. Pour voir Greywater de ses propres yeux, ne sachant pas / n'acceptant pas qu'elle
     n'existe pas physiquement.
  3. Le lieu est fictif, mais les émotions et souvenirs qu'elle y a vécus (lecture à son
     père malade) sont réels : la fiction a une valeur affective concrète.
  4. Il passait de "fiction = mensonge agréable" à l'idée qu'un lieu imaginaire peut
     abriter des souvenirs bien réels.
  5. La fiction est un refuge mental accessible quand la réalité se ferme — éloge du
     pouvoir consolateur du récit.
- **Expression** :
  - Attendus : lexique de la littérature et de l'imaginaire (*fiction, setting, to make
    up, meaningful, escapism, to relate to*).
  - Sujet B : thèse défendue avec exemples culturels précis, nuance possible.
  - Connecteurs : *arguably, for instance, furthermore, on balance*.
