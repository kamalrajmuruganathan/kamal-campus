# Bac — Compréhension et expression écrites (anglais)

**Durée conseillée : 1 h 30 — /20.**

## Document

> In the city of New Harbour, the mayor was proud of one number above all others:
> crime had fallen by forty per cent in three years. The reason, she said, was
> simple. On almost every street corner, a small camera watched over the citizens
> day and night.
>
> For many people, this was good news. Elderly residents said they finally felt
> safe walking home after dark. Shop owners no longer feared thieves. "If you
> have nothing to hide," one man told a journalist, "why should you worry about a
> camera?"
>
> But not everyone agreed. Leila, a law student, organised meetings in a local
> café. "Safety is not the only value that matters," she argued. "A city where
> you are always watched is not truly free. Privacy is not about hiding crimes;
> it is about being able to live without permission."
>
> The debate divided the town. Some said the cameras protected the weak; others
> said they slowly turned every citizen into a suspect. And a few quietly noticed
> something else: nobody had ever voted on the cameras. They had simply appeared,
> one by one, until they became part of the sky.
>
> "The most dangerous power," Leila warned, "is the one we stop noticing."

## Comprehension (10 points)

1. Why is the mayor of New Harbour proud, and what does she consider the cause? (2 pts)
2. What arguments support the cameras in the text? (2 pts)
3. Summarise Leila's point of view in your own words. (3 pts)
4. Explain the sentence: "nobody had ever voted on the cameras". Why is this
   detail important? (2 pts)
5. Comment on Leila's final warning. (1 pt)

## Written expression (10 points)

Choisis UN sujet et écris 150–180 mots :

- A. Write the speech Leila gives at a town meeting to defend privacy.
- B. "For a safer society, we should accept more surveillance." Do you agree?
  Argue your point of view.

## Corrigé indicatif

- **Compréhension** :
  1. La criminalité a baissé de 40 % en trois ans, grâce, selon elle, aux caméras à
     chaque coin de rue.
  2. Sentiment de sécurité des personnes âgées, protection des commerçants ; l'argument
     "si vous n'avez rien à cacher...".
  3. La sécurité n'est pas la seule valeur ; une ville sous surveillance constante n'est
     pas libre ; la vie privée = vivre sans autorisation.
  4. Les caméras n'ont jamais été soumises au vote : elles se sont imposées sans débat
     démocratique — enjeu de consentement.
  5. Le pouvoir le plus dangereux est celui que l'on cesse de remarquer / de questionner.
- **Expression** :
  - Attendus : lexique surveillance/liberté (*privacy, surveillance, freedom, consent,
    to monitor, trade-off*).
  - Sujet B : équilibre sécurité vs libertés individuelles, au moins deux arguments.
  - Connecteurs : *admittedly, nevertheless, consequently, in the long run*.
