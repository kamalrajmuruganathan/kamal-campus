# Bac — Compréhension et expression écrites (anglais)

**Durée conseillée : 1 h 30 — /20.**

## Document

> Tom had always been the slowest runner in his class. During PE lessons, he
> finished last, and some students laughed as he crossed the line, red-faced and
> out of breath. He began to hate Mondays, because Monday meant running.
>
> One autumn, a new teacher, Mr Abara, arrived at the school. He did not care
> about who was first or last. "The only person you should compare yourself to,"
> he told the class, "is the person you were yesterday."
>
> Tom decided to test this idea. Every evening after homework, he ran around the
> park near his house. At first he could only manage ten minutes before stopping.
> He wrote his times in a small notebook, like a secret diary of numbers.
>
> Slowly, the numbers changed. Ten minutes became fifteen, then twenty. He was
> still not the fastest boy in school, and he probably never would be. But in
> March, when the school organised a five-kilometre charity run, Tom finished it
> without walking a single step.
>
> He did not win a medal. Nobody carried him on their shoulders. Yet, as he
> crossed the finish line, he felt something new and quiet inside him: pride.

## Comprehension (10 points)

1. Why did Tom hate Mondays at the beginning of the text? (2 pts)
2. What advice does Mr Abara give the class? (2 pts)
3. How does Tom train, and how do we know he is serious? (2 pts)
4. Does Tom become the fastest runner? What does he achieve instead? (2 pts)
5. Explain the last sentence. Why does Tom feel "pride"? (2 pts)

## Written expression (10 points)

Choisis UN sujet et écris 120–150 mots :

- A. Write a diary entry as Tom on the evening after the charity run.
- B. "Competing against yourself is more important than beating others." Do you
  agree? Give reasons and examples.

---

## Corrigé indicatif

- **Compréhension** :
  1. Parce que le lundi = cours de sport, où il finissait dernier et où on se moquait
     de lui.
  2. Ne se comparer qu'à soi-même : "the person you were yesterday".
  3. Il court chaque soir dans le parc et note ses temps dans un carnet (rigueur, suivi).
  4. Non, il ne devient pas le plus rapide ; il réussit à courir 5 km sans s'arrêter.
  5. Il ressent de la fierté car il a progressé et surmonté ses limites, indépendamment
     du classement.
- **Expression** :
  - Attendus : prétérit du récit (sujet A), présent + modaux pour l'argumentation (B).
  - Vocabulaire : *effort, progress, to overcome, self-confidence, achievement*.
  - Connecteurs : *at first, little by little, in the end, however, therefore*.
