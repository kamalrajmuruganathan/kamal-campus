# Bac — Compréhension et expression écrites (anglais)

**Durée conseillée : 1 h 30 — /20.**

## Document

> When Daniel applied for his first job, he never met a human being. He uploaded
> his CV to a website, answered a few written questions, and recorded a short
> video of himself. A few days later, a polite email informed him that he had not
> been selected. No name was signed at the bottom.
>
> Behind that decision was not a manager, but an algorithm — a piece of software
> trained to sort thousands of candidates in seconds. The company was proud of
> it. "It is faster and fairer than humans," the director explained. "A computer
> does not care about your accent or your surname."
>
> But is that really true? Software learns from old data, and old data often
> contains old prejudices. If a company hired mostly men in the past, an
> algorithm may quietly learn to prefer men in the future. The machine is not
> cruel; it simply repeats the mistakes we taught it.
>
> Daniel eventually found a job — in a small company where a real person read his
> CV and asked him about his hobbies. "The interview felt human again," he said.
> "I could explain who I am, not just what my file says."
>
> Technology can help us decide. The question is whether we should let it decide
> for us.

## Comprehension (10 points)

1. Describe the recruitment process Daniel went through. (2 pts)
2. Why is the company proud of using an algorithm? (2 pts)
3. Explain the danger described in the third paragraph. (3 pts)
4. Why did Daniel prefer the interview in the small company? (1 pt)
5. Comment on the last sentence: what question does the text raise? (2 pts)

## Written expression (10 points)

Choisis UN sujet et écris 130–150 mots :

- A. Daniel writes a letter to a newspaper sharing his experience with automated
  recruitment. Write the letter.
- B. "Important decisions should always be made by humans, not by machines." Do
  you agree? Argue your point of view.

## Corrigé indicatif

- **Compréhension** :
  1. Il a envoyé son CV en ligne, répondu à des questions écrites et enregistré une
     vidéo ; aucune personne rencontrée.
  2. Le directeur estime que c'est plus rapide et plus juste : un ordinateur ignore
     l'accent ou le nom de famille.
  3. Un algorithme apprend sur des données anciennes qui contiennent des préjugés ; il
     peut donc reproduire des discriminations (ex. favoriser les hommes).
  4. Parce qu'un humain a lu son CV et l'a interrogé sur ses centres d'intérêt ; il a pu
     montrer qui il est.
  5. Faut-il laisser la technologie *aider* à décider ou *décider à notre place* ?
- **Expression** :
  - Attendus : lexique du travail et de la technologie (*applicant, bias, artificial
    intelligence, fairness, to rely on*).
  - Modaux d'opinion : *should, must, ought to*.
  - Sujet B : nuance attendue (avantages de rapidité vs risques éthiques).
